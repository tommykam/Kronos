Ten Under Ten

Every name on this list shares exactly one trait: a share price below $10. That is a formatting coincidence, not a thesis. Underneath it sit ten businesses with almost nothing in common — a $2.6 billion travel platform, a going-concern biotech, and a company that sold its entire revenue base six weeks ago.

ABSI Absci Corporation Platform bet

An AI protein-design platform that has stopped selling software and started building its own drugs. Judge it as a clinical-stage biotech with an unusually good balance sheet, not as an AI company.

Q2 revenue$0.3M
vs consensus$1.01M
Q2 net loss$33.2M
Cash 6/30$201.1M
Runway2H 2028
Avg target$13.78
Supports
  • $100M raise including $40M from Eli Lilly — validation and capital in one move, funding operations into 2028.
  • ABS-201 interim Phase 1 data showed a clean safety profile and a half-life of at least 65 days, pointing at infrequent dosing.
  • Two large consumer-scale indications: pattern hair loss and endometriosis.
  • Street is constructive — consensus Strong Buy, targets $10–$17.
Against

  • Hair-loss data is blinded interim. Unblinded efficacy is the real test and it hasn't happened.
  • Morgan Stanley moved to Hold in August, against a bullish crowd.
NextUnblinded HEADLINE Phase 1 efficacy for ABS-201 — the event that decides whether the $200M is funding an asset or an experiment.
ACOG Alpha Cognition Launch ramp

ZUNVEYL is the first new oral Alzheimer's treatment approved in over 15 years, and its launch curve is the cleanest execution story on this list. The question is whether cash lasts long enough to reach the profitability target.

Q2 revenue$6.0M
q/q growth+71%
1H total$10.3M
Cash 6/30$41.4M
FY26 opex$50–54M
Prescribers1,347
Supports
  • Revenue accelerating, not just growing: +40% q/q then +71% q/q.
  • Depth beneath the headline — bottles dispensed +37%, prescribers +26%, repeat prescribers +29%. Doctors are coming back.
  • Opex guidance was cut to $50–54M while revenue rose. Rare discipline in a launch.
  • Working capital ~$51.5M against only $6.4M of current liabilities.
Against
  • The arithmetic is tight: $41.4M cash against $50–54M of annual opex. Management's "12 months" rests on revenue continuing to compound.
  • Operating profitability is targeted for 2027 — a year away, and a financing may arrive first.
  • Trading at ~95% of its 52-week high, so the launch is already in the price.
  • Alzheimer's commercialization has broken larger companies than this one.
WatchQ3 revenue against the ~$8–9M the q/q trend implies, and any capital raise — the two are directly linked.
AGEN Agenus Going concern

A genuinely interesting immuno-oncology asset attached to a balance sheet that carries a going-concern warning. The science and the solvency are two separate bets and you cannot take one without the other.

Market cap~$120M
Cash 3/31$35M
Short-term debt$30M
New raise$85M
If warrants exercise$340M
OS readout2H 2028
Supports
  • BOT/BAL has treated roughly 1,300 patients — this is a mature clinical dataset, not a first-in-human story.
  • Targets MSS/pMMR colorectal cancer, a "cold tumor" where checkpoint inhibitors have historically failed.
  • The July placement was oversubscribed: $85M upfront, up to $340M with warrants.
  • Phase 3 BATMAN and neoadjuvant ROBBIN both running.
Against
  • A May SEC filing carried a going-concern disclosure. That is the most serious flag any company on this list has.
  • $35M of cash against $30M of short-term debt at Q1.
  • The $340M headline is mostly warrants — it only arrives if the stock rises, and it dilutes heavily when it does.
  • First overall-survival readout is 2H 2028. That is two more years of funding to find.
NextWhether the going-concern language clears from the next 10-Q after the placement. Until it does, dilution risk dominates the science.

What the list tells you as a group

Five observations that only appear when you look at all ten together rather than one at a time.

Share price is the only thing these have in common

Market capitalizations here span from roughly $120M (AGEN) to around $2.6B (GBTG) — more than a twentyfold difference — at nearly identical share prices of $7.49 and $9.46. A sub-$10 quote says nothing about size, value, or quality. It says the company chose not to reverse-split.

This is a sector bet, not a diversified list

Seven of the ten are healthcare or biotech. Those seven share the same risk factors — FDA decisions, biotech funding windows, sector-wide sentiment — so they will tend to fall together. Holding all ten gives you far less diversification than the count of names suggests.

Two of ten make money

Only DHX and GBTG are profitable. Six of the remaining eight are funding operations from cash reserves, and their most important number is not revenue but runway. For AGEN that runway carries a going-concern warning; for ABSI and IRD it stretches to 2028 and 2029 respectively. That difference matters more than any pipeline detail.

It screens on momentum, whether or not that was intended

Six names sit within 15% of their 52-week highs, and GBTG is essentially at its high. Buying this list means buying strength that has already been rewarded. The two names furthest from their highs — NUVB at ~70% and IRD at ~71% — are the ones where a value argument is even available.

At least one row is describing a company that no longer exists

The list's own disclosure notes the data is as of the previous Friday, but CPIX has a deeper problem: it closed the sale of its entire marketed portfolio on 4 August and paid a $1.50 special dividend on 31 July. Its trailing revenue, margins and price history all refer to a business it no longer owns. Always check for corporate actions before trusting a screener row.
